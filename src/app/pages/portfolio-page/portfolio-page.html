09Why AI governance matters

Rules make power answerable.

Governance creates expectations for accountability, safety standards and transparency across systems that cross borders. It must also deal with different legal and cultural contexts, unequal access to infrastructure and the need for global cooperation.

10AI ethics across cultures

Ethical expectations can differ across cultures, societies, legal systems, communities and economic contexts. Shared principles such as dignity and safety matter, but a single framework may not capture every local meaning or lived risk.

Global principles+Local / cultural context=Responsible AI approach
11Who benefits from AI?

Access is part of ethics.

Responsible AI should ask who has access to infrastructure and computational resources, who owns the data, who receives economic benefits, whose communities are represented, who has digital literacy, and who can influence governance. These differences can exist without needing unsupported statistics; they are questions of power and participation.

Responsible AI — Our core message

AI power + human judgment + ethical awareness + verification + accountability = responsible AI

Responsible AI balances innovation with truth, safety, fairness, human dignity and accountability.

03Our reflection

01 · What we learned

We learned that generative AI ethics is not only about whether a tool works. It is also about how claims gain trust, how creative labor is recognized, how power is distributed and who remains responsible.

02 · Ethical challenges

The hardest questions resisted simple answers: disclosure can support trust but may not prevent harm, safeguards can protect people but also overreach, and efficiency can create value while placing pressure on creative workers.

03 · How AI changed our thinking

Using AI as a drafting and exploration aid made the difference between a plausible response and a defensible one more visible. We had to slow down, compare ideas and decide what belonged in the portfolio.

04 · Human judgment

Human judgment remained necessary for context, tone, fairness and the consequences of presenting an idea to an audience. A generated answer could start a discussion, but it could not finish the ethical reasoning.

05 · Responsible use

We treated AI as an assistive tool, reviewed outputs, kept the final interpretation with the student team and avoided presenting demonstrations as real evidence.

06 · Team learning

The shared framework helped connect different topics. Truth, creativity, power and responsibility became linked questions rather than separate sections.

07 · What we would improve

We would add more verified external references, more perspectives from affected communities and further accessibility testing before treating the portfolio as complete research.

Final takeaway

Generative AI is powerful not because it removes human responsibility, but because it challenges us to use human judgment more carefully.
